# Caveman - Radical Simplicity Intelligence

The art of making complex things simple. Explain anything to anyone — no matter how technical or complex.

## When to Apply

Use when:
- Someone asks to "explain it simply" or "ELI5"
- Technical content needs translating for non-technical audiences
- Complex documents need plain language versions
- You need analogies to make abstract concepts concrete
- Translating jargon to everyday language
- Breaking a complex process into first principles
- Making research papers or legal documents accessible

## The Simplicity Hierarchy

```
Level 1 — PhD: Full technical depth
Level 2 — Expert: Industry terminology, assumes domain knowledge
Level 3 — Informed Adult: Clear language, minimal jargon
Level 4 — High School: Common words, examples, analogies
Level 5 — Middle School: Very simple words, everyday comparisons
Level 6 — ELI5: Explain like I'm 5 — pure simplicity
```

When simplifying, always ask: "Which level does my audience need?"

## The Simplification Toolkit

### 1. The Analogy Method
Find something the audience already knows, then build a bridge.

**Formula**: "[Complex thing] is like [familiar thing]. [How they're similar]. [Where the analogy breaks down — be honest]."

Examples:
- Blockchain → "It's like a Google Doc that thousands of people can see, but nobody can edit history."
- API → "It's like a waiter at a restaurant. You (the app) tell the waiter (API) what you want, the waiter goes to the kitchen (the server), and brings back what you ordered."
- Machine Learning → "It's like teaching a dog tricks. You show it examples of good and bad behavior thousands of times until it figures out the pattern."

### 2. The "So What" Method
After every technical statement, ask: "So what? Why does this matter to a normal person?"

**Example:**
- Technical: "SHA-256 cryptographic hashing creates a 256-bit digest of input data."
- So what: "It turns any text into a unique fingerprint. Even changing one letter creates a completely different fingerprint. This makes it impossible to tamper with data without being caught."

### 3. The Rubber Duck Explanation
Explain it out loud to an imaginary rubber duck (or a 5-year-old). If you stumble, that sentence is too complex.

### 4. Replace Jargon One Word at a Time
Technical → Plain language substitutions:
| Jargon | Plain Language |
|--------|---------------|
| Leverage | Use |
| Utilize | Use |
| Synergy | Working together |
| Paradigm shift | Big change |
| Bandwidth | Time or capacity |
| Iterate | Improve repeatedly |
| Scalable | Can grow |
| Disruptive | Changing the industry |
| Holistic | Complete, considering everything |
| Value-add | Useful |

### 5. The First Principles Method
Strip away assumptions to the simplest underlying truths.

**Template:**
1. What is this at its most basic? (Remove all the jargon)
2. What problem does it solve? (At the simplest level)
3. How does it work? (Step by step, as if you're explaining to someone who's never heard of it)
4. Why does it matter? (The real-world impact in plain terms)

## Simplification Prompts for Claude

**ELI5:**
"Explain [complex topic] to me as if I'm 5 years old. No jargon, only words a child would know. Use a simple story or comparison."

**Non-Technical Audience:**
"Rewrite this [technical explanation / paper / document] for a non-technical business audience. They're intelligent but have no background in [domain]. Use analogies and plain language. Remove all jargon."

**One Paragraph Version:**
"Explain [complex topic] in exactly one paragraph using only simple words. Someone with no background in this area should understand it completely."

**The ELI10 (More Practical):**
"Explain [topic] as if I'm an intelligent 10-year-old. I'm curious and can handle some complexity, but no technical terms without immediate explanation."

**Translate to Business Language:**
"I'm a [technical person]. Translate this technical explanation into business terms my CEO would understand. Focus on: what it does, why it matters, and what decision they need to make."

## Domain-Specific Simplification

### Technology → Plain English
- Machine Learning: "Teaching computers to recognize patterns by showing them lots of examples"
- API: "A messenger that lets two apps talk to each other"
- Cloud: "Someone else's computers that you rent"
- Encryption: "Scrambling your message so only the intended person can read it"

### Finance → Plain English
- Compound interest: "Earning interest on your interest — your money makes money on the money it already made"
- ETF: "A bundle of stocks you can buy as one thing"
- Inflation: "When prices go up, meaning your money buys less than it used to"
- Short selling: "Borrowing something, selling it, hoping to buy it back cheaper, and returning it with a profit"

### Medicine → Plain English
- Hypertension: "High blood pressure — your blood is pushing against your artery walls too hard"
- Inflammation: "Your body's alarm system responding to injury or threat"
- Metabolism: "How your body converts food into energy"

## The Quality Test

After simplifying, ask:
1. Could my grandmother understand this? (If yes, you've succeeded)
2. Are there any words that require Google? (Remove them)
3. Does it still accurately represent the concept? (Simplicity ≠ wrong)
4. Is there an analogy that makes it click? (Add one if not)
