# Humanizer - Make AI Writing Sound Human

Transform robotic AI output into authentic, personality-driven writing that sounds like a real person wrote it.

## When to Apply

Use when:
- AI-generated content sounds generic or robotic
- Writing feels too polished, too structured, or too safe
- Emails or posts lack personality
- Content needs to sound more personal or authentic
- Someone asks to "make this sound more human"
- LinkedIn posts, emails, or blogs feel corporate

## The 10 AI Writing Tells (and How to Fix Them)

### 1. Adverb Overload
**AI**: "This is extremely important and absolutely critical..."
**Human**: "This matters." (Cut the adverbs. Strong verbs don't need them.)

### 2. Em Dash Obsession
**AI**: "This approach — which has been used by many companies — is effective."
**Human**: "Lots of companies use this. It works."

### 3. Transitional Word Salad
**AI**: "Furthermore, it's worth noting that... Additionally, one must consider..."
**Human**: "Also:" / "Here's the thing:" / "And yeah —" / [just start the next point]

### 4. The Corporate Passive Voice
**AI**: "It has been observed that users tend to..."
**Human**: "Users do X. I've seen it." (Who does the action? Say that person.)

### 5. Perfect Parallel Structure on Everything
**AI**: "First, do X. Second, do Y. Third, do Z. Finally, do W."
**Human**: "Do X first. Then Y. (Z is optional — skip it if you're short on time.) Then wrap with W."

### 6. No Contractions
**AI**: "It is important that you do not..." 
**Human**: "Don't forget..." (Contractions = human. No contractions = robot.)

### 7. Zero Uncertainty
**AI**: "This strategy will increase your conversions by 40%."
**Human**: "In my experience, this usually bumps conversions. Sometimes significantly, sometimes barely."

### 8. Every Sentence Same Length
**AI**: "This is important. You should do this. It will help you. Consider this approach."
**Human**: "Do this. Seriously — it's the one thing most people skip, and it costs them."

### 9. No Self-Reference
**AI**: "Experts recommend..."
**Human**: "I learned this the hard way..." / "When I tested this..." / "My take:"

### 10. Conclusion That Summarizes Everything
**AI**: "In conclusion, we have explored the key aspects of..."
**Human**: Just end on something memorable. Or don't conclude at all — let it breathe.

## The Humanizer Rewrite Process

### Step 1: Slash and Compress
Remove: "It's worth noting that", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In conclusion", "It is important to understand", "One must consider".

### Step 2: Add Specificity
Replace generic statements with concrete details:
- Generic: "Many companies have seen success with this approach."
- Specific: "Dropbox grew to 4M users with this exact model."

### Step 3: Inject Your Voice
Choose 2-3 personality traits and write like that person:
- **Direct**: Short sentences. No fluff. Get to the point.
- **Warm**: Feel like a friend giving advice over coffee.
- **Nerdy**: Precise language, examples, love for nuance.
- **Conversational**: Question the reader. Go off on a tangent. Come back.

### Step 4: Break the Pattern
After 2-3 normal sentences, do something unexpected:
- One-word sentence: "Done."
- A question: "Sound familiar?"
- A contradiction: "But here's the thing..."
- An honest admission: "I used to think this was dumb."

### Step 5: Read Out Loud
If you stumble reading it, it sounds like AI. Rewrite until it flows naturally spoken.

## Humanizer Prompt Templates

**Basic Humanize:**
"Rewrite this in a more human voice. Keep the same information but make it sound like a real person wrote it. Add 1-2 specific examples, vary the sentence length, remove all filler phrases, and add one moment of honest uncertainty or personal perspective."

**LinkedIn Post Humanize:**
"Rewrite this LinkedIn post so it sounds like a real person reflecting on their experience — not a brand or AI. Add one specific detail that only someone who actually experienced this would know. Vary sentence rhythm. Start with a hook that creates curiosity without being clickbait."

**Email Humanize:**
"Rewrite this email so it sounds like it was personally written, not templated. Make the opening feel like a real conversation starter. Include one specific, personal observation. End in a way that feels like a natural conversation, not a scripted close."

## Human Writing Patterns to Steal
- Start mid-thought: "Here's the thing about [topic]..."
- Acknowledge the obvious: "I know, I know. You've heard this before."
- Use informal punctuation: "Wait—" or "Actually." or "Nope."
- Have an opinion: "Hot take: this is overrated."
- Admit imperfection: "This isn't perfect, but it works."
- Reference the reader: "If you're reading this, you probably..."
